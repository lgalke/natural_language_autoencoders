# Idea (2026-10-05): enforce L/H separation by verbalizing the streams in separate calls ("split AV")

- **Proposal (user):** one AV, called twice per row, once with only z_L and once with only z_H (stream tag in the prompt, one marker, per-stream injection adapter); the AR reads each field with its own head; reward = individual reconstructions plus the sum term, shared by the two samples of a pair. Separation would hold by construction (the swap control then must change the output). The existing SFT data supports it without new teacher calls (two independent explanations per row, one per call). Cost: new prompt template and sidecar, `train_av_sft`, `train_rl` (two generations per pair), `eval` and tests changed; SFT plus RL about 8 h of compute; roughly a day of work.
- **Concerns (reasoning, not measured):** (i) z_L and z_H are strongly anti-correlated (cos about -0.8), so both explanations would describe the same dominant direction; separation by conditioning is not separation of content; (ii) the individual MSE terms barely reward stream-specific detail; the sum term does (errors must cancel) but two independent samples cannot coordinate as the joint AV does; (iii) credit assignment: both samples get the same sum reward.
- **Decision rule:** first measure the stream-specific variance with `python -m nla.hrm.lh_variance --base base.parquet --limit 20000` (ridge R^2 of z_L from z_H on held-out documents, shared-normalised; stream-specific share = 1 - R^2). Rough rule: R^2 above about 0.9 means little stream-specific variance and the data (not the architecture) is the limit; R^2 below about 0.7 means a split AV has something to find. The thresholds are my guesses.
- **Other options, cheaper than the split AV:** (a) partial-patch judge (patch only ẑ_L with true z_H, or only ẑ_H with true z_L at H_in@2) for the current model: tests stream-specific function without retraining; (b) cross-reconstruction (L text to z_H and H text to z_L; code exists, untested); (c) verbalize the sum s (what H reads) and the residual instead of z_L and z_H.

# Best-of-N by reconstruction consistency (implemented 2026-10-06, not yet run)

- **Method:** `eval --best-of N` generates the greedy completion (candidate 0) plus N-1 temperature-1 samples (top_k disabled, top_p 1) per row and keeps the candidate whose AR reconstruction is closest to the row's gold vectors (recon loss with the eval weights). At inference the vector is available, so this is a legitimate "sample, then verify with the AR" step; the reported FVE is selection-biased (selected on the metric). Independent checks: marked-token accuracy (the selection never sees the token), the printed `best_of_report` (token accuracy of greedy vs selected vs a random candidate vs oracle, and recon loss greedy vs selected), the judge KL (uses the selected reconstruction, so it also improves partly by construction), and reading the text. Mock-model tests in `tests/hrm/test_best_of.py`; cached as `<output>.gen_<split>_bo<N>.pt`; the dump gets a `bo` field (chosen index, per-candidate losses and quote correctness).
- **Model choice:** run on E7 `final` (`ckpt/rl_tokw/final`): E8 hides hallucination from its reward and is not better on any held-out measure.
- **Command:** `python -m nla.hrm.eval --eval-parquet iid=eval_iid_clean.parquet ood=eval_ood.parquet --av-ckpt ckpt/rl_tokw/final --ar-ckpt ckpt/rl_tokw/final --norm-stats-json norm_stats.json --limit 100 --max-new-tokens 400 --best-of 8 --dump-samples samples_tokw_final_bo8.jsonl --output eval_tokw_final_bo8.json --run-judge --mean-from rl.parquet`. # E9: split AV (separate L and H calls), planned 2026-10-06

- **Idea (user):** one verbalizer called twice per row: the L call sees only z_L (z_H zeroed) and writes the L field, the H call sees only z_H (z_L zeroed) and writes the H field; the two fields of a pair are scored together by the unchanged AR and the same reward (sum plus individual terms). # BUG FOUND: the AR readout was misaligned under left padding (2026-10-11); AR ceiling tool

- **Bug (details and fix in [pitfalls](/observations/pitfalls.md)):** in `eval.py` and `train_rl.py` the AR read `hidden[attn.sum - 1]` from batches tokenized with LEFT padding (the tokenizer is switched to left for generation), so every row shorter than the longest in its batch was read at a padding position or mid-text. Found while building `nla.hrm.ar_ceiling`; checked with the real Qwen tokenizer. AR-SFT used right padding and was correct. **All FVE, judge-reconstruction and RL numbers logged before 2026-10-11 (E4 to E13) used the misaligned readout, and the RL runs also trained the AR (online update) and computed rewards with it.** Size of the effect: not yet measured.
- **Fix:** `model.last_real_index` (last attended token; correct for either padding side); `eval --critic-readout last|legacy` and `train_rl --critic-readout last|legacy`, default `last`; `legacy` reproduces the old behaviour (`.gen_*_legacyreadout.pt` cache name in eval). Unit tests in `tests/hrm/test_critic_readout.py`; 49 tests pass.
- **Consequences to resolve:** (1) quantify with `ar_ceiling` (variants `gen_last` and `gen_legacy` on the same generated texts; `gen_legacy` should reproduce the eval FVE of the checkpoint, a sanity check); (2) a checkpoint whose AR was trained under the legacy readout may score LOWER with the fixed readout, so the honest comparison needs the AR (and the RL that trains it) rerun with `--critic-readout last`: candidate E14 = the E12 recipe (`ckpt/av_split_sft_pos` start, 500 steps) with the fixed readout; (3) the conclusions that do not use the AR readout stand (probes, the stated-fact dissociation across four SFT runs, which comes from the AV's text only); conclusions that use FVE or the judge of reconstructions (FVE plateau, split vs joint parity, E8/E10/E13 reward effects, best-of-N) are provisional until re-measured.
- **AR ceiling tool (`nla.hrm.ar_ceiling`, tests in `tests/hrm/test_ar_ceiling.py`):** AR forward on the generated texts of a dump with variants: as generated (last / legacy readout), true token, true position, both true facts, facts only (generated or true), prose only; paired FVE differences to `gen_last`. It answers how much FVE a perfect extraction of the stated facts would add and what the prose contributes. Command: `python -m nla.hrm.ar_ceiling --dump samples_split_pos_final.jsonl --eval-parquet iid=eval_iid_clean.parquet ood=eval_ood.parquet --ar-ckpt ckpt/rl_split_pos/final --norm-stats-json norm_stats.json --output ceiling_e12.json`. **Results:** TODO.
